    Reading the board

    Holes are named by row letter and column number, so j5 means row j, column 5. Columns are printed along both long edges every five holes.

    In each column, rows a to e are connected to each other, and rows f to j are connected to each other. The center gap keeps the two halves apart, which is why resistors, buttons, and the buzzer bridge it.

    Every ground goes to the − rail beside row a (the one with the blue line). Each rail is 25 connected holes in 5 groups of 5, so any hole in that rail works if the exact one is hard to reach.

    Only the LEDs and buzzer have a direction. The LED's long leg is +, and the buzzer's + is marked on top. Resistors and buttons work either way round.

    Each color's LED and button share a wire color, black is the ground wire from the UNO, and orange is the buzzer. White is left as a spare. Any color works electrically.

    The short ground links are 100 Ω resistors (brown, black, black, black, brown), each bridging row a to the − rail hole right below it. They add a little resistance, so the LEDs run slightly dimmer than with plain wire. The buttons aren't affected.
